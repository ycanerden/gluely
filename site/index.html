Know the cost before you spend a credit

Before a scrape or an enrichment run, Gluely works out the bill from your own prices. A missing price stays missing and is never counted as free. Your agent can read the estimate, but only a person can approve it. If a price or the volume changes, it needs approving again.

$ gluely cost job-switch
# example, with made-up prices
Check the list  412 checks           $4.12
Find contacts   1 to 2.1 lookups     $0.06 to $0.12
Send messages   2.1 to 4.1 messages  $0.00

Total           $4.18 to $4.24
Per person      $2.06 to $4.06

→ Within your $150 limit. A person needs to
  approve it before anything spends credits.

A tool library that ranks nobody

Every tool is listed by the job it does, in alphabetical order. Yours come first, and any tool you type in works.

    Gluely checks the work

    It never sends a message and never calls a model. It holds your rules: your voice, your exclusions and your plays, each with a kill rule. Every command also speaks JSON, so your agent can use it too.

    $ gluely check draft --channel email \
    --text "Hi Jane, I hope this finds you well"
✘ Draft breaks your voice rules
  ✘ banned phrase: "hope this finds you well"

$ gluely check company rival.example
✘ rival.example is excluded: competitor

    It learns how you write

    Every decision and every edit you make is logged, with who made it and why. Your agent reads those lessons before it writes the next draft. When you keep changing the same thing, Gluely shows you, and you can turn it into a rule that holds for good.

    $ gluely learn
# example
voice 3 open, belongs in context/messaging/voice.md
  I never use exclamation marks
  Changed "Quick question" to "One question"

You keep removing  "quick" 2x

$ gluely check draft --channel email \
    --text "Quick thought on the new team"
✘ Draft breaks your voice rules
  ✘ banned phrase: "quick"

    Every correction becomes a test

    Early on, reply rates are mostly noise, but your corrections aren't. Tell the brain it got something wrong and it keeps the lesson. Each context change is then tested against the last one, so you see it when the brain gets worse.

    $ gluely test
Brain tests   context 319e1d3
✔ Rule tests   4/4
✔ Agent tests  6/7 correct
  ✘ icp-warm-founder: answered hot, expected warm

    Your brain stays yours

    Your context, plays and tests live in your own private repo. Gluely has no servers, so your data stays in your own stack. It is free, MIT licensed, and recommends tools without ever requiring one.
